I have a folder full of abandoned projects. For years, I saw this as failure. Now I see it as a library of lessons learned.

## Project Graveyard

Here's what I discovered going through old repositories:

### The "Everything App"

I tried to build a productivity app that did email, tasks, notes, and calendar. It was too ambitious. I learned that scope creep kills projects before they launch.

**Lesson**: Start smaller. Solve one problem well.

### The Social Network for Niche Community

Built for three weeks before realizing I had no idea how to get users. Had all the features, zero distribution strategy.

**Lesson**: Think about distribution from day one.

### The Perfect Todo App

Spent two months building the "perfect" task manager instead of using existing tools. It never shipped.

**Lesson**: Don't build solutions looking for problems. Scratch your own itch, but ship fast.

## Abandonment is Not Failure

Sometimes quitting is the smartest thing you can do. The opportunity cost of continuing a doomed project is real.

These abandoned projects taught me:
- What motivates me (and what doesn't)
- Which technical problems I enjoy solving
- How to recognize dead ends faster
- The importance of shipping imperfect things

Now I abandon projects guilt-free. Each one is practice, not a promise.
